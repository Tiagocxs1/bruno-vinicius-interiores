What the Design Process Looks Like, Step by Step

From the first call to the final reveal — an honest, plain-language walk through how a residential project actually runs.

Clients tell me the scariest part of a project is the unknown: how long it will take, who does what, and where the money goes. Here is the process I use, in plain language, so you know what to expect.

1. Discovery

We talk through how you live, work, and host — the routines, the habits, the things that annoy you about the space today. I listen for the feeling you want the room to carry. This stage costs you nothing but an hour, and it tells me whether we’re a good fit.

2. Design

Concept, mood, materials, and layout — developed together and refined until it feels inevitable. You will see plans, palettes, and material samples, and we will go back and forth until the design matches the way you actually live. This is where the vision takes shape, before a single wall is touched.

3. Development

Technical drawings, sourcing, and coordination with builders and artisans, managed end to end. You receive clear budgets and a realistic timeline, and I handle the suppliers so you are not the middle person. The unknowns are resolved here, on paper, where they are cheap.

A good process moves the problems onto the paper early, where they are cheap to solve — not onto the build, where they are expensive.

4. Delivery

Installation and styling, down to the last object. The space is handed over ready to live in — not staged for a photograph, but arranged for your week. This is the moment everything quietly clicks, and it is the reason the process exists.

What to ask any designer before you start

  • How do you handle the budget — and what happens if it shifts?
  • Who manages suppliers, builders, and timelines?
  • What do you do when a chosen product is discontinued?
  • How will we communicate, and how often?
  • What does the fee cover, and what is outside it?

The right process is the one that keeps you informed without burying you in detail. You should always know what stage the project is at, what happens next, and what it costs.
